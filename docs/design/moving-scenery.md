# Scenery that moves: the drifting cross and the trains

Two maps put moving solids on the board. This note records the shape of the change and why it is shaped that way.

## Intended behaviour

- **Drifting cross** (`drift`): the wrapping board of `wrap` with a cross of two walls on it. The walls start on the board's edges — the round opens looking like the classic room — and slide across the board at a fixed velocity each, turned back by the edge they reach, so the cross wanders the way a screensaver logo does. Because the board is a torus, the cross always divides it into exactly one rectangular room, and that room drifts: riders have to keep moving with it, through the open edges when the room crosses them. Overtime closes the edges exactly as it does on `wrap`; the cross keeps drifting.
- **Trains** (`trains`): the classic walled board with a new railway every round (rules `fuse-p2p-55`; before that, two fixed loops and three trains). Two to four loops of track wind across the board — each the outline of a random blob of grid cells, so a loop doubles back in hairpins and wraps round bays — and five to seven trains of three to five cars run on them at 5 to 7.5 units a tick, every train on a loop the same way round at the same speed. Loops never cross themselves or each other and never run through a rider's spawn corridor. The track is paint; the cars are solid squares that kill on contact and stop shells and bullets. A train that rolls onto a rider kills it.
- Wandering walls and trains are **permanent**: a blast does not clear them and the overtime walls do not crush them. A map is its movers; a railway with nothing on it is the classic arena. In overtime the trains keep running through the closing band and across what is left of the field, so a level crossing inside the walls is as deadly as ever.

## Design

A mover is an ordinary `Obstacle` with a `motion` (`games/fuse-riders/src/engine/scenery-motion.ts`). Reusing the obstacle machinery means every existing interaction — rider death and the shield bounce, shell reflection, bullets stopping, pickups and gates keeping clear, bots planning around — works unchanged; the change is a step per tick and a few rules about what a mover is exempt from.

- **State, not derivation.** A mover's position and its `motion` (velocity, or distance round the loop) are state. A checkpoint restores a train where it was and every replica advances it with the same arithmetic (`+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `Math.sqrt`; no time-based recomputation, no trigonometry). The tracks are state too (`GameState.tracks`), laid by `layRailway` (`games/fuse-riders/src/engine/railway.ts`) in `prepareRound` from the round's seeded stream, after the spawn shuffle and the sampled scenery, and reach a screen as `view.tracks`.
- **Laying a railway.** Track runs along a 16×8 grid of 80×75 cells inside the classic walls. A line grows a blob of cells from a random seed cell toward a random size, a cell at a time; a cell at an arm's tip is four times likelier to be grown than one that fattens the blob, which is what makes lines wind. A cell is admitted only if the blob's cells round it form one unbroken run that is not a lone corner neighbour, so the blob never gets a hole or a pinch and its outline is one simple loop; corners are cut by 24 units. Cells within reach of a spawn corridor are never grown, the finished loop is checked against the corridors exactly, and each line claims its cells and the ring round them, so lines stay a cell apart. Trains go one per line first, then each to the line with the most track per train while every train keeps a five-car slot and two cars of headway. If no line fits at all, the railway falls back to two plain loops in the outermost columns, which no spawn corridor reaches.
- **One phase.** `moveScenery` runs after `fitField` and before anything rides, so the whole tick is judged against where scenery stands at the end of the tick. Per-tick displacement (at most 7.5 units) is small against a car's 32, so nothing tunnels; a rider crossing just behind a car is spared, which is the generous side.
- **Let-out rule.** A rider is let out of scenery it already stood in (immunity can lapse inside a rock). For a mover that is judged where the piece stood _before_ its step (`ctx.sceneryBefore`), so a car that has just rolled onto a rider is not something the rider was inside of, and the rider dies.
- **Open edges.** Scenery is now met across an open edge as trails are: the rider's step and a gun ray's leg are tested against each image of a piece that the wrap brings within reach (`movementImages`, `legImages`), and the bot planner lists the images within its reach. Shells already reflected off the image-shifted `solid` list. On a walled board the image list is the single identity offset, so those paths are bit-identical to before.
- **Checkpoint guard.** `motion` is admitted in its two shapes only, and only on `wall` and `train`; a `wall` stands only on `drift` and may span the board (half extents to half the board), other kinds stay bounded to a quarter; a `train` stands only on `trains`, on a loop of the round's railway, within its length; the railway itself stands only on `trains`, holds at most `MAX_TRACKS` loops of 3 to `MAX_TRACK_POINTS` points each, with every point far enough inside the board that a car centred on it stays on the board, and every loop with length to run along; a bouncing wall's step fits the room it bounces in, and `reflect` clamps as well, so no admitted state can put a mover off the board.
- **Rendering.** Standing scenery stays baked into the floor pass; movers are painted every frame from the interpolated view. The rails are baked. A car's lights face along the track; the lowest id of a train is its locomotive (cars are laid head first).

## Tradeoffs

- Trains do not bend rider planning: bots see a car where it is now, not where it will be. They re-plan every tick, which is enough to avoid most, not all, of them, and the expresses (as fast as a fresh rider) catch more; that is deliberate — bots get no privileged physics.
- Lines never cross. A level crossing between two lines would need trains to wait for each other or pass through each other; keeping lines a cell apart gives up that picture for trains that can never collide. The complexity comes from each line's shape instead.
- The railway keeps clear of the riders' spawn corridors, not of where riders go next: nobody starts in front of a train, and after the opening run anything goes.
- Tracks are carried in the checkpoint rather than recomputed from the seed and round. They are a few hundred numbers at most, and carrying them keeps the rule that whatever the simulation reads is state, with no derivation that a later rules change could silently break.
- The cross's initial place and velocity are fixed rather than seeded, so every round of `drift` opens the same way. A seeded start would be a one-line change in `fixedScenery` and a rules bump.
- Neither map is in `rotate`. The rotation is what a default room plays, and both maps change what a round asks of a rider; a room opts in, as it does for `wrap` and `cross`.
