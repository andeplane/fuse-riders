# Fuse Craft

See the [current tech tree](TECH_TREE.md) for all research branches, structure and particle unlocks, costs, timings, and construction requirements.

The [expansion and balance report](BALANCE_REPORT.md) records the strategy tournament, reproducible command replay, browser evidence and remaining balance limits.

The [latest AI adaptation comparison](verification/established-artillery-2026-09-27/README.md) covers 210 map/opening/seat cases: established short-range armies can build safe artillery positions when they cannot advance directly. Ten 900-second timeouts remain, down from twenty under the preceding policy.

Fuse Craft (previously Neural Defence) is a real-time territory RTS: grow a connected network from a brain across the whole map, mine **Biomass** and **Insight**, research, and direct a fixed pool of attack particles. Win by destroying every rival brain, or by **dominance**: hold 40% of the map in a duel (35% with four players), and 1.5 times any rival's territory, for a full minute. Contested cells, touched by two networks, count for nobody.

New players can start with **Tutorial** (a coached first game on Slate Basin that advances as you do each thing) and **How to play** (rules, structures, research, powerups, strategies and controls, generated from the engine catalog; also under **?** during a match). When a match ends, **Match report** explains what decided it and charts territory, economy, army and damage over time, with a scoreboard, each player's research path and the key moments. [Strategies](STRATEGIES.md) documents the openings and their counters from the AI benchmark. The first skirmish version is under review in [PR #417](https://github.com/andeplane/fuse-riders/pull/417). Known open work is tracked in [TODO.md](TODO.md). It is available in the local preview; it has not been merged or deployed. The existing `fuse-craft` URLs and internal identifiers remain stable.

Choose **Single player → Start** for the default player-versus-AI match on Cortex Crossing (29 × 23): you and the AI start in opposite corners around a walled central lobe. It is the most balanced map across openings in the benchmark and seats up to four, so it is also the default for new rooms. Close Quarters remains for a fast game with nearby brains, where early pressure dominates. The AI builds, researches and routes particles through ordinary commands, with the same resources and travel rules as you. Destroy its brain to win; losing your brain ends the match. Play again restarts directly with the starting camera. Synaptic Reach remains available for a longer economic opening, alongside the open sandbox and scripted combat lab. Online play is described below.

The tactical minimap shows the actual blocked terrain, deposits, structures and camera view. Click it to move the camera. Ground art is flat and traversable; raised rock and deposit sprites correspond to the same map cells used by building and routing rules. Building silhouettes are distinct across world, ghosts and command buttons.

Choose **Single player → Watch AI vs AI** to select two openings and watch a live match. Inspect either network, move the camera with the minimap, or select a player card to focus its brain. **Find battle** jumps to current fighting or the nearest opposing networks without taking over manual camera control. The bottom panel shows each opening, brain health and construction losses; Restart repeats the chosen matchup. Watching cannot issue build, research or attack commands. Both AIs use the ordinary deterministic game rules and the existing session clock. See the [watch-mode verification](verification/watch-mode-2026-09-27/README.md) and [developed live-battle captures](verification/battle-framing-2026-09-27/README.md).

Choose **Multiplayer** to play Versus online. **Create room** gives a four-character code, a link and a QR code to share; friends open the link or enter the code under **Join**, type a name and take a seat. The room's host picks the map (two, four or six players), the opening its bots play and whether powerups spawn, can add or remove bots, and starts once two players up to the map's capacity are ready. Every human and bot owns a network; destroy every other brain to win. The host can return everyone to the lobby or call a rematch, and a page opened with the room link rejoins as the same player. Rooms run through the shared room service (`pnpm dev` serves them locally at `/fuse-craft/`; and Cloud Run serves them in production, where `EXTRA_GAME_IDS` names `fuse-craft` since 2026-10-01). See [Versus and powerups](verification/versus-powerups-2026-09-30/README.md).

**Powerups** spawn one minute in and every 40 seconds after, up to two at a time, on open ground roughly equidistant from the brains, and fade after 45 seconds. The first network to touch one claims it; while two networks touch it, it stays contested. **Nutrient cache** gives +150 biomass and +50 insight, **Regrowth** heals every structure except the brain by 40% of its health, **Growth surge** doubles build speed for 30 seconds and **Synaptic frenzy** doubles every weapon's fire rate for 20 seconds. They are on by default in Player vs AI, Watch and Versus, and every AI opening races for nearby ones once it has no weapon to place.

Settings include sound, volume and reduced motion. Sound starts muted by default; `?mute` forces silence regardless of the saved setting. Short synthesized cues follow resolved building, research, combat and match-result events. They do not drive the simulation.

The opponent's opening defaults to **Random**: you scout what it builds and counter it, and the match result reveals what it played. You can also pick Balanced, Pressure, Economy, Siege, Relay, Defensive or Swarm; Play again keeps a chosen opening and draws a new random one. Room bots open at random by default too, each drawing its own opening from the match. All use normal commands, resources and timing; they adapt to enemy artillery and answer a creep spread with Spore towers. See [Strategies](STRATEGIES.md) for what each does and what beats it.

The map picker also offers **Close Quarters** (earlier contact), **Open Synapse** (open flanks), **Twin Pass** (two passages through a rock ridge), **Scarce Reach** (fewer safe deposits), **Twin Hemispheres** (a fissure with three bridges), **Synapse Islands** (islands joined by one-cell synapses; the dominance map), **Cortex Crossing** (four seats around a walled central lobe) and **Grand Cortex** (six seats). The four newest maps come from `scripts/fuse-craft-maps.ts` with exact hex symmetry and a per-seat fairness report. All eleven are validated maps used by the strategy tournament and bundled for online rooms. Rock and deposit graphics always come from their real tile categories.

Each player has one builder and 128 reusable attack particles. **Neurons sprout** like creep: a neuron queued beside the connected network grows by itself, without the builder. A network grows one sprout at a time at first and one more for every 50 cells of territory, up to four, so expansion snowballs with map control. Neurons carry no weapon; they claim ground (their own cell and the six around it), mine adjacent deposits and carry supply. Territory pays a trickle of biomass. The builder raises towers, which are upgrades of neurons. Disconnected structures stop mining, claiming and firing. Five towers offer different roles:

- **Pulse:** sturdy, strong firepower at medium range.
- **Siege:** fires exactly three traversable hex steps away, with a two-step blind spot. Costly, fragile and slower between volleys; support it with close-range weapons.
- **Relay:** cheaper, quicker construction and frequent smaller volleys.
- **Bastion:** durable close-range defense, unlocked by Growth; artillery can outrange it.
- **Spore:** lobs a pod at the densest enemy cluster in reach; it bursts on impact and every enemy structure beside the target takes half the hit. Unlocked by Growth, it is the answer to dense creep and neuron-heavy openings.

Growth also unlocks the **Harvester**. Build it beside deposits for extra extraction; bonuses do not stack on a deposit. It conducts the network but has no weapon or Charge order. Construction uses each building's real durability and retains damage on completion.

Press **Q / Particles** to choose Pulse, Heavy or Swift profiles. Heavy hits harder but travels and recovers slowly; Swift reinforces and recovers quickly but deals less damage per shot. The choice applies when a particle returns to or departs from the brain; existing frontline and in-flight profiles do not change instantly. Research Growth, Excitation and Conduction, then Ballistics or Resonance to unlock specialist towers and profiles. Exact costs, requirements and timing live in the [engine catalog](../../games/fuse-craft/src/engine/catalog.ts).

The full-width battlefield sits between a thin resource bar and a compact bottom command dock. The six command slots mirror **Q W E / A S D**. The top row is **Particles / Build / Research**. Build, Research and Particles replace those slots in place; A goes back and D pages larger catalogs. Choose Build → a structure, then click or tap its location. A translucent ghost follows desktop hover; red marks illegal placement. Esc or S cancels placement. While placing, arrow keys move the target and Enter confirms an armed placement. Otherwise arrow keys scroll the camera while held and Shift+arrow keys move the selection. Resting the mouse at the board's edge, or on the outermost pixels of the screen even over the top bar and dock, scrolls too and keeps scrolling while it rests there (switch it off in Settings), pressing and dragging on the minimap keeps the camera on the pointer, and **F** or the ⛶ button puts the game itself in full screen where the browser allows it (Esc leaves), so the mouse cannot leave the window and edge scrolling works on every side.

Select a friendly frontline structure and press **D / Charge** to give it maximum attack-particle priority; press again to clear the order. The slider permits finer allocation. Particles must travel through connected links before that structure can fire automatically at an enemy in range. Strong positions need supply and redundant connections, not just more towers.

Hold **Shift** while clicking build locations to queue several plans of the chosen type. **Shift+Enter** also keeps keyboard placement active. An ordinary click/Enter places one final plan and exits; Esc or S cancels placement without cancelling earlier plans. Invalid or duplicate sites add no job and keep placement active.

Select your brain for **S / Auto expand**. It stays enabled while waiting for biomass, the builder and valid ground. Manual plans take priority while they can connect to your network. Turning it off finishes the current construction but starts no further automatic jobs.

Clock overlays show construction and research progress. Hover or focus commands for requirements; tap grey commands on phones for the same explanation. Eligibility and missing-research explanations come from the shared rules catalog. Legal queued plans wait for resources or support.

Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, or use one-finger pan and two-finger pinch on phones. Secondary-click drag (right button or Mac two-finger click-and-drag) and Control-click drag always pan, including while placing a building; they never place or select. The battlefield suppresses the browser context menu. Zoom-in stops at three screen pixels per board unit, close enough to inspect a fight but not so close one building fills the screen; zoom-out stops at a readable scale that fills both viewport axes, including after resize or rotation; use the minimap for an overview. There are no Fit map or +/− buttons. Continuous mossy basalt ground replaces repeated hex slabs, and matches begin at a closer, readable camera scale. Bright curved links show actual friendly connections; disconnected fragments have dashed links and dormant bodies. Supply orbits, builder/particle journeys, arrival pulses and attack flashes animate presentation only. Reduced motion is available in Settings.

Networks are living tissue. Every neuron is drawn procedurally from its location: one of four cell types (stellate, pyramidal, bipolar, granule) in one of three tones of its team colour, so no two neighbours look alike, and the same cell looks the same in the world, the placement ghost and the inspector. A paid neuron grows inside a throbbing cocoon; when finished it sprouts, and its dendrites sway, carry signal sparks and reach out toward each connected neighbour, which reaches back. New links grow in from the older end. Beneath each network a raised, veined creep slab spreads out from every structure as it is built and slowly recedes when a structure is destroyed or cut off, in the spirit of Zerg creep. Brains and towers rise from raised mounds of the same tissue, rooted into the creep, and brains breathe. A destroyed neuron tears open into droplets and a fading splat, and each brain sends a slow heartbeat ripple through its creep.

The ground itself is alive: faint blood vessels branch beneath every map and blood pulses through them on a shared heartbeat; biomass pods breathe and puff spores, insight crystals hover and sparkle, and every mined deposit streams motes into the structures harvesting it.

A GPU light layer is added over the battlefield through the same camera: nuclei, brains, towers, deposits and cocoons glow; a signal runs along every live axon; particles in transit carry light; spores drift over the arena; and shots throw hit flashes and bouncing sparks, destruction throws embers, and construction releases rising motes, each timed to the shot's arrival. A bloom pass blurs that light and adds it back so bright sources glow into their surroundings, team-coloured light pools under each network, a warm key light matches the cast shadows, and a vignette grades the view. Browsers without WebGL2 render the same game without the light layer. All of this follows authoritative state and presentation time only; reduced motion keeps only steady glows and shows the network at rest. See [organic network and light](verification/organic-light-2026-09-30/README.md).

Sound effects are synthesized in the browser (no audio files) and are on by default; the first click starts them, as browsers require. Interface actions click, open and close; placing, dispatching the builder, finishing a building or research, a stalled or rejected order each have a cue. Every weapon sounds different (Pulse towers zap, siege towers boom, spores pop), shields shimmer, a kill crunches and a lost building sounds worse, and a destroyed brain rumbles. An alarm sounds at most every eight seconds while a rival hits your network. Powerups chime as they appear and when claimed, dominance has a rising swell (or a warning when a rival has it), and the match opens with a heartbeat and ends with victory, defeat or draw. Board sounds pan with the camera and fade off screen; rapid fire is thinned so a large fight stays readable. Spectators hear the battle but no personal cues. **🔊** in the match bar or **M** mutes and unmutes; Settings also has volume. `?mute` silences a page load without changing the saved choice.

Use `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` and `pnpm dev` from the repository root, then open the server's printed `/fuse-craft/?mute` URL. Vite development uses `/games/fuse-craft/?mute`. Add `&debug` for optional instant construction/research; both default off and retain costs and travel. Dev servers choose a free port.
